How is it that WhatsApp has become the defacto method for global, one to one
communication e incluso para grupos?

One can imagine that WhatsApp offers something that works well and serves the
needs of people to connect. It is beyond dispute that it is a good product.
The question has to be this: how is it that this great product
is paid for? If WhatsApp is "gratis," then who paid the developers? Who pays
the costs of maintaining the WhatsApp infrastructure? Who pays for the data
bandwidth needed to send millions of messages zinging around the globe?

The obvious answer is that advertisers pay, and pay handsomely. They pay to
display an advertisement to you. They pay when you interact with their
advertisement, that is, when you "convert." The deeper answer is much more
interesting.

Few are going to picket in Plaza Independencia outside of the executive
offices asking for an end to WhatsApp "Gratis." "We want to pay! We want to
pay!" Never mind that the reality is that we would pay very little.

We are much
more valuable as consumers incited to earn and spend that as direct customers.

One response has been to run to our legislatures demanding privacy laws.
In response, the internet companies deploy armies of lawyers and lobbyists
to ensure that these laws may be circumvented through disclosure and agreement.
Thus, the "Terms and Conditions" and "Privacy Policies" that you agree to
without reading become longer and longer, more and more indescypherable to
you or me. You didn't read them before. You are less and less likely ever to
read them. You agree to them the same.

Everybody is happy: the public, lawyers, legislators, the corporations. The
public because they have the illusion of control. The lawyers because they are
well paid to navigate the legislative mine field that now exists. The
legislators because they can point out to their constituents how well they are
looking after them while ensuring further campaign financing from corporate
interests. The corporations because they can continue to fleece the public.

It is entirely our own fault. We want to rely on it, that it works well. We
want it to be private. We want it to be free.  We want our cake and eat it
too. No wonder we are such suckers!

All of this seems perfectly innocent. It's just money. We don't really mind
looking at an advertisement once in a while. The ad's can be very useful
to us. We do see things that are of interest.

Where is the dark side? There are at least two.


Firstly, the data-net has a picture of you that is highly personal. It knows
more about you than your closest friends, even your family, even your spouse.

Secondly, the data-net uses powerful machine classification techniques to
place you in all sorts of categories. Some of these categories might cause
you to be excluded from opportunities or conversely, chosen or targetted for
opportunities. The machine classification creates classes that may be used
to exclude or include you in parts of public life. We'll illustrate this
in the following.

Thirdly, there are three interest groups that have access to this profile
of you in the data-net. The first is the Meta corporation that collects,
processes and disseminates the data. The second is customers of the Meta
corporation who pay for access. The third is virtually carte blanche access
granted to the state in the name of law enforcement.

Note that we pick on Meta. This is because we are talking about WhatsApp.
All of this applies equally to Google. You tell them which web sites you
have accounts with through Google sign-in. You tell them how often and when
you access those web sites. You allow them to read your mail, including mail
that you receive. Google has trackers everywhere to follow you just about
anywhere you go with a browser. If you use the Google Chrome product, they
gather even more data about your browsing behavior and habits. You probably
allow Google Maps to keep track of where you (or rather, your phone, that
is your proxy) have passed the time or travelled to.

Why are these evil? Let's begin with the third. Who has access? Who can
know you better than you know yourself?

None of these parties is accountable to you, not to you personally nor very
much at all. None of them need to disclose to you that your classifications
were accessed, when, by whom nor even what those classifications are.
Do they have you classified as gay, trans, or married with mistresses?
What kinds of political alignments have they classified you for? Socialist?
Libertarian? Anarchist? Conservative? What kind of political talk gets your
attention? All of this they know. Who accesses this information is of no
account to you.

We have already discussed privacy laws.
Never mind the extensive disclosures that the EU imposed for privacy.
You have stated that you have read, understood, and accepted them.
You accepted that there is very little information WhatsApp does not collect.
(They make a big deal of not having access to the content of your communications,
thus claiming that your use of WhatsApp is "private.")
You accepted that there are few with whom they would not share your data, in
particular, whatever the state requires.
